Aaj Ki Naari: India’s Growth, But at What Cost?

Beyond economic progress: exploring women’s health, access, agency, safety, and development in India.

A woman’s development cannot be understood through one health number or one economic statistic. This dashboard places nutrition, menstrual health, digital access, money, property, choice and safety side by side. All figures are from the NFHS-5 survey (2019–21), not 2026 statistics, and show association, not cause.

What does this mean for India’s development?

Interpretive framework, not an NFHS-5 finding.

“Development is not only about how much an economy grows. It is also about whether people can live healthier lives, access opportunities, make decisions, and exercise their rights.”

Health, schooling, money, safety and a say in decisions can reinforce one another, and national averages can hide large gaps between states, rural and urban areas, and rich and poor households. In this data, for example, thinness falls as wealth rises while overweight rises, so women can face different kinds of nutritional disadvantage. Survey data show where gaps exist; they do not prove why, and they should guide policy questions rather than quick conclusions.

Sources and definitions

Main source: IIPS & ICF (2022). National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5), 2019-21: India, Volume I. Mumbai: IIPS. Report link. Secondary reference: PRS Legislative Research, Vital Stats: NFHS-5 (28 Dec 2020), phase-1 states only, not used for figures here.

Limits: surveys are self-reported; spousal violence figures do not capture all violence against women; a bank account does not mean control of the money; owning a phone is not digital literacy; contraceptive use alone does not show autonomy.
